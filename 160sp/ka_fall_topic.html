Brief meta-review

24 papers in the corpus; 8 lab studies, 12 field studies, 4 meta-analyses. Effects are robust in lab settings (Hedges' g ≈ 0.45, replicated four times), more variable in the field. Two studies have failed to replicate the headline effect in distinct populations.

Why these papers are ranked high

Lab studies with VR-tractable measures and at least two replications appear first. The Bratman paper appears with a "partial" flag because its core comparison (nature walk vs urban walk) is reproducible in VR, but its sgPFC fMRI outcome is not — a substitute behavioural measure of rumination would be needed.

Annotations — design commentary

For the instructor's review of the Fall journey. Not student-facing copy; sits at the bottom so the page above reads as a student would experience it.

What this surface is

Surface 3 of the nine-surface Week-1 journey: the per-topic page, reached from a Did-You-Know card via "Explore this topic." "Nature views and cognitive recovery" is shown here as a worked example topic. The page is the bibliography-building workspace and is a logged-in surface supporting a deferred-commitment workflow — the student need not pick a topic on first visit. The wireframe source is ka_week1_wireframe_2026-05-17.html §Surface 3.

The meta-review and the anchor-paper table

The meta-review at the top is a short distinguishing summary of the literature, not just a paper list. The anchor-paper table is the bibliography-differentiator artefact; its columns are chosen for what a Week-1 student needs at a glance — design type, sample size, outcome measure, VR-tractability with a substitution flag if partial, and replication count — and rows are sorted by suitability for the 7-to-10-week project rather than by citation count. Each paper title is a hot link to the canonical article-viewer page (ka_article_view.html?id=PDF-XXXX); per DK 2026-05-18 the table should not be the terminal view of a paper.

Short-list versus pick

The two-column short-list / pick action replaces the single "Pick" checkbox of an earlier draft (DK 2026-05-18: support the student building a short-list and not deciding immediately). Short-list persists a paper to the student's collection across sessions without committing; pick commits the paper as a Week-1 anchor. Both require the student to be logged in. The short-list card above the commit card shows the persistent collection of topics, DYK findings, and individual papers — items added from the DYK browser, this page, or the article viewer all land there.

What is mocked

The meta-review text, the six anchor papers, the placeholder PDF IDs (PDF-0142, PDF-0007, and so on), and the three short-list rows are all illustrative. Production should generate the meta-review from the corpus's already-extracted fingerprints and substitute real corpus IDs for the placeholders. The VR-tractability flag per paper, the VOI badge, and short-list persistence are all backed by infrastructure that does not yet exist.

Dependencies

Meta-review generation requires fingerprint extraction to be complete or near-complete on the anchor papers; the paper-quality build provides the substrate. The per-paper VR-tractability flag requires the substitution-agent infrastructure (Surface 4). The suitability-for-project ranking requires a rule set — CW proposes lab over field for VR-tractability, within-Ss over between-Ss for smaller N requirements, and at least two replications. Short-list persistence requires a logged-in user model and a per-user collection table; Shibboleth authentication is deferred to Fall 2026, with a local profile keyed to the student's UCSD email acceptable until then (schema sketch: short_list_items(user_id, item_type, item_id, added_at, note)). The hot anchor-paper links require the article-viewer page to accept query-string IDs and degrade gracefully when a paper has not yet been extracted; Track-4 students are designing that page, and CW will coordinate the URL contract.

Open decisions

Whether the topic page shows every corpus paper on the topic or only the top 8–12 anchors — CW recommends the top 8–12 with a "show all 24" expander, otherwise the table becomes a tour rather than a tool. Whether a student may commit to a topic without selecting any anchor papers — CW recommends no, because the rationale paragraph the deliverable demands depends on having read some of the literature.
