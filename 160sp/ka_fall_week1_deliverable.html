One topic for the VR experiment you will build over the next ten weeks. It must be operationalisable in VR.
What to include: the construct of interest, why VR is the right method, and one expected finding from your anchor papers. A word-count indicator appears as you type.
Your starter bibliography (4–6 papers)

List four to six papers. Mark which are anchors — papers you understand well enough to summarise in three sentences — and which supply a substitute construct or measure.

  • Kaplan & Kaplan (1989) anchor
  • Berman, Jonides & Kaplan (2008) anchor
  • Bratman et al. (2015) substitute construct
  • Hartig et al. (1991)

The list above is an example bibliography for the "nature views and cognitive recovery" topic. Replace it with your own papers.
Self-check before submission
  • My topic is VR-tractable, or I have a substitution path.
  • I have read at least three of the anchor papers.
  • My rationale names the construct I will study.
  • My rationale states why the topic is worth studying (its value of information).
  • I have read what VR can measure.
  • I have skimmed the methodological pitfalls page.

Annotations — design commentary

For the instructor's review of the Fall journey. Not student-facing copy; sits at the bottom so the page above reads as a student would experience it.

What this surface is

Surface 9 of the nine-surface Week-1 journey: the Week-1 deliverable capture. It is reached from Surface 3 (the topic page) or Surface 4 at the end of Week 1, and is the explicit exit condition of the journey. The wireframe source is ka_week1_wireframe_2026-05-17.html §Surface 9.

Content provenance

The four capture blocks — topic field, rationale textarea, starter bibliography, and self-check — are taken from the wireframe mock. The example bibliography (Kaplan & Kaplan 1989, Berman/Jonides/Kaplan 2008, Bratman et al. 2015, Hartig et al. 1991, van den Berg & ter Heijne 2005) and its anchor/substitute badges are the wireframe's worked example for a "nature views and cognitive recovery" topic. The six self-check items are reproduced verbatim from the wireframe checklist.

Why the exit is observable

The deliverable capture is the explicit exit of the Week-1 journey. The Track-4 design principle is that a journey without a defined exit produces drift, so the self-check checklist makes the exit observable. The six checklist items mirror the exit-criteria panel on Surface 1, closing the loop. Two items cross-link out: "what VR can measure" to ka_vr_measurability.html (Surface 6) and "the methodological pitfalls page" to ka_methodological_pitfalls.html (Surface 7).

Dependencies and what is mocked

The form fields render but are not wired: there is no persistence, no word-count indicator behind the rationale textarea, and the Submit CTA is a placeholder (href="#") with no backend. Per-student progress tracking is the core missing dependency — the system needs to know which student has submitted, when, and what they submitted, and submitting must mark Week 1 complete and unlock the Week 2 landing page on the following Monday. The instructor view that aggregates all Week-1 deliverables across the class, with topic-distribution statistics, is a separate surface not in scope here. The page is structurally and content-complete but operationally static until per-student state lands.
