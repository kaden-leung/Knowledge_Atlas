Findings

Did you know? Office workers in spaces with views of vegetation show 12% faster recovery from cognitive load tasks than those in views-of-built-environment offices (Kaplan & Kaplan, 1989; replicated three times in lab, twice in field).

Did you know? The blue-light component of daylight measurably advances circadian rhythm within a single morning's exposure — by ~30 minutes per 10,000 lux-hours (Brainard et al., 2001; meta-analysed in 2018).

High Marginal value of a new study in this area is high because:

Annotations — design commentary

For the instructor's review of the Fall journey. Not student-facing copy; sits at the bottom so the page above reads as a student would experience it.

What this surface is

Surface 2 of the nine-surface Week-1 journey: the Did-You-Know topic browser, reached from Surface 1 via "Browse Did-You-Know cards." Each card surfaces one finding; the new Week-1 affordances are the VR-tractability badge and the topic-family filter. The wireframe source is ka_week1_wireframe_2026-05-17.html §Surface 2.

The two filters

The topic-family filter and the VR-tractability filter at the top let the student narrow without abandoning the browse mode. Cards that are not VR-tractable are shown greyed and explicitly flagged rather than hidden — CW recommends shown-greyed for pedagogical reasons, so the student sees what is in the broader literature even if they cannot test it themselves. The "Contested" badge marks findings with a mixed replication record; for Week 1 a contested finding is often a good target but requires careful framing.

The card CTAs

Each card carries three or four outbound CTAs (DK 2026-05-18 revision). "Explore this topic" opens Surface 3, the per-topic page where the student builds a bibliography. "Add to short-list" persists the topic to the student's logged-in short-list without leaving the browser, so candidates can be collected and decided later rather than committed at first glance. "Find related papers" jumps to Surface 4 (evaluate-a-paper) pre-populated with this card's anchor citation. Contested cards carry a fourth CTA, "Why is this contested?", opening the contested-finding rationale on the validities showcase.

What is mocked

The four cards use illustrative findings; production should show 8–12 cards with a load-more affordance. The VR-tractability flag per card is editorial until the substitution-agent infrastructure (Surface 4 dependency) exists — an instructor or CW tags each card by hand. The VOI badge depends on the VOI operationalisation work (Surface 5 dependency). The card design borrows from the existing DYK work in data/ka_payloads/did_you_know.json and the writing contract at docs/DID_YOU_KNOW_SCIENCE_WRITING_CONTRACT_2026-05-16.md.

Dependencies

The topic-family filter requires a canonical topic-family taxonomy; the existing crosswalk has the topics, but whether the five families shown here are the right grouping is itself a design decision. The VR-tractability flag depends on the substitution-agent infrastructure. The VOI badge depends on the VOI operationalisation document (docs/VOI_OPERATIONALISATION_THINKING_2026-05-17.md, forthcoming). Nothing on this page is wired to the live corpus yet.

Open decisions

How many cards to show at once — the mock shows four; production should probably show 8–12 with a load-more affordance, with card height tuned tall enough to read the finding without expanding and short enough to scan eight at once. Whether non-VR-tractable cards should be hidden by default or shown greyed — CW recommends shown-greyed; that decision is reflected here.
