From admissible to chosen

Your topic survived admit-mode in Week 1 because at least one VR-tractable measure exists. The typical situation by Week 3 is that multiple admissible measures are on the table, each with a profile of psychometric strengths and weaknesses that only surface when the candidates are laid out side by side. This surface is that comparison. Every measure shown is admissible — the choice between them turns on the trade-offs.

Worked example — choose a measure: attention-restoration topic

The student has chosen the attention-restoration topic. Four measures index the construct and are VR-feasible in a 7-to-10-week class envelope. The choice involves hardware, time, sensitivity, and exposure to demand characteristics.

Measure Indexes what Hardware required Time per participant Strengths Weaknesses to flag
Pupillometry
marker of mental effort and arousal		 Cognitive effort during a probe task before and after restoration exposure. VR headset with integrated eye-tracking (Quest Pro, Vive Pro Eye, Varjo); Quest 2 cannot ~25 min Physiological, hard to fake. Reliable in repeated measures. Robust literature. Confounded by ambient luminance; baseline drift across long sessions; cannot run on Quest 2 (lab hardware gating).
SART
Sustained Attention to Response Task (Robertson et al. 1997)		 Sustained-attention performance (lapses, omission errors) before vs after restoration exposure. Any VR headset; no peripheral required. ~12 min per administration Well-validated. Sensitive to attentional state. Hardware-agnostic — Quest 2 fine. Trivial to deploy. Floor/ceiling effects depending on dosage and stimulus rate. Participants find it boring; motivational confound across sessions.
PRS
Perceived Restorativeness Scale (Hartig, Kaiser & Bowler 1997)		 Self-reported perception that the environment was restorative. Any VR headset; questionnaire admin within or outside VR. ~5 min Trivial to administer. Established psychometric profile. Maps directly onto Attention Restoration Theory's four-factor structure. Subjective; vulnerable to demand characteristics. Measures the participant's reportable experience, not the construct itself. Should not be used alone; pair with a behavioural measure.
ANT
Attention Network Task (Fan et al. 2002)		 Three independent attention networks (alerting, orienting, executive) before vs after restoration exposure. Any VR headset; precise timing required. ~30 min Decomposes attention into three independently measurable networks; restorative effects on the executive network are predicted, on alerting are not. The decomposition is itself the finding. Long; subjects fatigue. VR-administered ANT has only a handful of validation studies; reaction-time precision in a head-mounted display is below CRT baseline.
Substitution skill recommendation

For a 7-to-10-week class project, the skill ranks the SART + PRS pair first — combined behavioural and self-report converge on the construct, the pair is hardware-agnostic, and together they take ~17 min. Pupillometry is preferred if the lab has Quest Pro or equivalent (gold-standard physiological signal). ANT is most informative scientifically but expensive in subject time and harder to deploy reliably. PRS alone is discouraged — too vulnerable to demand characteristics for a credible class deliverable.

The ranking rationale is shown in plain language; each ranking element points back to the relevant entry on the methodological-pitfalls page.

Annotations — design commentary

For the instructor's review of the Fall journey. Not student-facing copy; sits at the bottom so the page above reads as a student would experience it.

What this surface is

Surface 4b of the nine-surface Week-1 journey: the choice-mode counterpart to Surface 4. A student has settled on a topic in Weeks 1-2; the topic survived admit-mode (Surface 4, ka_evaluate_paper_for_vr.html) because at least one VR-tractable measure exists. By Week 3 the student must commit to a stimulus and a measurement protocol, and the typical situation is that multiple admissible measures are on the table. This surface is the side-by-side comparison. It is reached from Surface 4 after admit-mode succeeds, or from the topic page at the Week-3 transition. The wireframe source is ka_week1_wireframe_2026-05-17.html §Surface 4b.

Why it is a Week-3 surface

CW treats Surface 4b as the Week-3 hand-off rather than a Week-1 surface proper. It is wireframed alongside Week 1 only because Weeks 2 and 3 are not yet wireframed, but it conceptually belongs to the Week-3 measure-selection step of the COGS 160 Fall arc. The phase strip on this page therefore marks Weeks 1 and 2 done and Week 3 current.

Admit-mode versus choice-mode

Where Surface 4 answers can I run a study in VR on this topic?, Surface 4b answers given that I can, which measure will I commit to?. The two questions are sometimes conflated in student thinking. Separating them sharpens the methodological reasoning: admit-mode is a corpus-wide question (does any path exist?); choice-mode is a within-project question (which path do I want?). The substitution skill is invoked here in ranking mode rather than binary mode — every visible measure is admissible, and the choice between them turns on the trade-offs. The full argument is in docs/USER_JOURNEY_COMMENTS_RESPONSE_2026-05-18.md §5.

Why a ranking, not a single answer

The recommendation is a ranking, not a single "best" measure. The choice-mode skill exposes its reasoning and never abstracts the trade-offs behind one answer, because the trade-offs are pedagogically the most valuable part of the surface. Each measure's profile is composed from two sources: the corpus-internal record (how the measure has been used in our papers, with what reliability) and the corpus-external psychometric record (Cronbach α, test-retest, known cultural specificity).

Dependencies and what is mocked

The choice-mode skill requires the planned construct-to-measure-to-VR-feasibility knowledge base (UJ-2 in TASKS.md); the attention-restoration example above is hand-built for the wireframe, and the real surface depends on panel input on confidence display and refusal criteria. The hardware-gating annotation (Quest 2 vs Quest Pro vs Vive Pro) requires a small machine-readable hardware profile per measure — CW proposes a single JSON file data/vr_hardware_profile.json that the skill reads. The cross-links to the methodological-pitfalls page require that page to exist with per-pitfall anchors; Surface 7 is itself blocked on panel review of the green/yellow/red tagging (UJ-6 in TASKS.md). The comparison table, the recommendation card, and the three CTAs are all static until those dependencies land.

Open decisions

Whether the surface should offer a "Commit" action that wires the selected measure into the student's project record, or remain advisory — CW recommends advisory-with-export for the first build, with the commit-action waiting for the student-project-record schema. When the student picks a measure the skill ranks lower, whether the system should push back or accept silently — CW recommends a soft pushback ("you chose ANT over SART+PRS; ANT is longer and harder to deploy — confirm you have lab time?") with a confirm-anyway option, since patronising the student is the worse failure.
