Methodological pitfalls a 7-to-10-week project can still respect

Even a short course project should know the standards it cannot meet, and should meet the ones it can. This page covers the threats beyond Cook & Campbell's classic four validities, drawn from the post-2010 methodological-reform literature — and the standards a short project can realistically hit.

Threats your project should anticipate

These are threats you may not be able to eliminate inside a 7-to-10-week envelope, but you should know they exist — and acknowledging them honestly is itself a methodological standard.

Cook & Campbell's four validities

Internal, external, construct, and statistical-conclusion validity — the classic framework that organises the threats any study faces. It is the standard frame Cognitive Science methodology courses use, and the foundation every other item on this list builds on.

See the four-validities showcase A standalone walk-through of the canonical taxonomy
Garden of forking paths Gelman & Loken 2014

Every analytic decision you make after seeing the data inflates false positives — even if you only ever run one analysis, the analysis you chose depended on what the data looked like. Mitigation: pre-specify your analytic plan before you collect data.

HARKing Kerr 1998

Hypothesising After the Results are Known — presenting a post-hoc explanation as if it were the prediction you set out to test. Mitigation: write down your hypothesis before you run the experiment.

P-hacking Simmons, Nelson & Simonsohn 2011

Undisclosed flexibility in data collection and analysis — trying variations until something crosses p < .05. Mitigation: report all measures, all conditions, and all exclusions.

WEIRD-sample issues Henrich, Heine & Norenzayan 2010

Your UCSD sample is WEIRD — Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich, Democratic — and not representative of humans in general. Mitigation: state this in your discussion; do not over-generalise.

Multiple comparisons

If you run twenty tests, you expect one significant by chance alone. Mitigation: pre-specify your primary hypothesis(es); correct for the rest or treat them as exploratory.

Standards a 7-to-10-week project can meet

Unlike the threats above, these are standards you can and should hit. For a short course project, every one of these is realistic.

  • Pre-registration. Free, takes an hour, and prevents the garden of forking paths. Use OSF or AsPredicted.
  • Random assignment. Required for causal inference. Use a coin flip, a script, or the experiment's randomiser.
  • Pre-specified primary outcome. One measure, one hypothesis, written before data collection.
  • Pre-specified analysis plan. Include it in your pre-registration. Departures from it should be flagged in the write-up.
  • Honest sample reporting. Who, how many, exclusions, demographics, dropouts.
  • Effect-size reporting with CI. Not just p-values. Cohen's d for two-group comparisons; partial η² for ANOVA.
  • Open materials and data. Deposit your A-Frame scenes, your analysis script, and your deidentified data on OSF.
