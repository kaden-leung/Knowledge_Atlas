Evaluate a paper for your VR experiment

Two paths through the evaluator

Paste a DOI or paper title below. The system first checks whether we already hold the paper in the research corpus, then takes one of two paths.

Path A — the paper is in the corpus

We already have the paper's fingerprint, theory tags, and IV/DV extraction. The admit-mode evaluation runs against those existing records and returns immediately.

Path B — the paper is not in the corpus

You upload the PDF. A four-step V7-Lite ingest pipeline classifies the paper, checks topic fit, extracts the IV and DV, and maps the DV against the VR-measurability taxonomy before evaluating.

Paste a paper to evaluate

DOIs resolve directly to the structured record. A free-text title runs a corpus search. If the paper is not in the corpus, you will be asked to upload the PDF.

Original design Field observational; 46 patients; gallbladder-surgery recovery; window-view vs brick-wall view.
Outcome measures Length of stay (days); analgesic doses; nurse-rated mood notes.
VR-tractable as-is? No Field setting, clinical population, and multi-day exposure are all outside the scope of a 7-to-10-week VR study.
Substitution paths The substitution agent identifies the paper's construct as stress-restorative effect of nature views. Three VR-tractable analogue designs are available in the corpus:
  • Lab analogue 1: Berman, Jonides & Kaplan (2008) — backward digit span before/after a VR nature walk vs a VR urban walk. VR-tractable
  • Lab analogue 2: Hartig et al. (1991) — Necker cube plus self-reported mood after VR nature exposure. VR-tractable
  • Lab analogue 3: van den Berg & ter Heijne (2005) — combined backward digit span and mood ratings, larger N. VR-tractable
7-to-10-week feasibility With substitution to the Berman et al. or van den Berg et al. design, yes. Sample size is achievable with class-internal pilot subjects, and the VR scenes are already templated.
VOI on this topic High Two failed replications in non-WEIRD samples; a fresh lab-VR run with a UCSD undergraduate sample contributes meaningfully.

When V7-Lite rejects an upload

A student uploads a paper on default-mode network connectivity during meditation. The classifier identifies it as empirical-fMRI; the embedding similarity to the nearest corpus topic ("attention restoration") is 0.41, below the 0.55 threshold. The system rejects the paper as out-of-scope, with this rationale:

"This paper is on neural correlates of meditation, a topic outside the current Atlas corpus on architectural cognition. The three closest corpus topics — attention restoration, mindfulness in built environments, and stress recovery — share some construct vocabulary but not the same intervention domain. Consider either picking from these closest corpus topics, or proposing your paper as a new-topic seed (instructor approval required)."

Is this a worthwhile topic to study?

The Value-of-Information panel appears alongside every evaluation. It answers, in plain terms, whether a new study on this topic would move the field.

VOI · Nature views and cognitive recovery

High The marginal value of a new study in this area is high because:

  • Two failed replications in non-WEIRD samples; a fresh sample contributes.
  • VR-mediated nature exposure has only three published runs; dose-response is unsettled.
  • No published study has tested the same effect with the new SR-MoCA cognitive battery; this is a methodological opening.
VOI = marginal expected reduction in uncertainty per study, ranked across topics.

Annotations — design commentary

For the instructor's review of the Fall journey. Not student-facing copy; sits at the bottom so the page above reads as a student would experience it.

What this surface is

Surface 4 of the nine-surface Week-1 journey: the admit-mode entry point for students who arrive already holding a paper — from a course, an outside reading, a recommendation — and want to know whether they can build their VR experiment around it. The system answers four questions. Are the paper's outcome measures VR-tractable as-is? If not, what substitute measures could probe the same construct? Do other papers in the corpus already test something similar in VR? Does the topic survive the 7-to-10-week project constraint? Surfaces 2 and 3 are the entry points for students who do not yet have a paper. The wireframe source is ka_week1_wireframe_2026-05-17.html §Surface 4.

Admit-mode versus choice-mode

The substitution skill is invoked twice in the COGS 160 Fall pipeline. First in admit-mode — this surface, Week 1 — answering whether a VR-tractable substitute exists for the paper's outcome measure, yes or no. Then again in choice-mode — Surface 4b, ka_choose_measure_for_vr.html, Week 3 — answering, given several admissible measures, which one the student should pick and what the trade-offs are. The two modes share the same knowledge base but expose different affordances. The distinction follows DK's 2026-05-18 comment that pruning and choice are distinct stages; the full argument is in docs/USER_JOURNEY_COMMENTS_RESPONSE_2026-05-18.md §5.

The two-path architecture

The two-path branching is the single most important addition from DK's 2026-05-18 review. The previous draft assumed every paper a student wants to evaluate already exists in the corpus — an unsafe assumption given that students bring papers from outside readings and the broader literature their professors recommend. Path A handles the in-corpus case: the system already has the paper's fingerprint, Toulmin warrants, theory tags, and IV/DV extraction, so the evaluation runs immediately against those records. The Ulrich (1984) example shows Path A. Path B handles the not-in-corpus case: the student uploads the PDF and a four-step ingest-decision pipeline runs — paper-type classification, an embedding-based topic-fit check against a calibrated cosine threshold, IV/DV extraction, and a VR-suitability mapping of the DV against the measurability taxonomy. Below-threshold uploads are rejected as out-of-scope with an explanatory rationale; plausible uploads with no topic match are offered as new-topic seeds for instructor review. The Lee, Park & Schultz (2025) example shows a Path B admit-with-substitution; the meditation/default-mode-network card shows a Path B rejection.

The V7-Lite pipeline

V7-Lite is new infrastructure. It runs the minimum subset of stages needed for the admit-mode evaluation — fingerprint extraction, IV/DV identification, methods extraction, and VR-suitability mapping — while the full V7 pipeline (visual cropping, science-writer summary, full PNU extraction, Toulmin warrant decomposition) is queued asynchronously. The student's evaluation returns within minutes; the full ingest lands hours later and incrementally upgrades the article-viewer page for the uploaded paper. The rejection path must be explanatory rather than terse: a student who gets a rejection should understand why their paper falls outside the corpus and what they could do instead.

Dependencies and what is mocked

The substitution agent itself does not yet exist. Building it requires (a) the instrument registry to carry construct-operationalisation information uniformly across the corpus, (b) a ranking heuristic over candidate substitutes, and (c) an explanation generator that produces the prose evaluation. This is a substantial Codex prompt and should be the most urgent of the new builds. VR-tractability tags per measure require the VR-measurability content (Surface 6) in machine-readable form; the quick-reference table in 160sp/ka_vr_measurability_content_2026-05-18.md includes short codes for that purpose, and a JSON sidecar should be derived from it. The 7-to-10-week feasibility check requires a small codified rule set (clinical population → no, multi-day exposure → no, fMRI → no, lab analogue with class-internal sample → yes). The V7-Lite ingest pipeline is owned by Codex and awaits a follow-up sprint prompt. The 0.55 cosine threshold shown is illustrative and needs per-topic calibration. Both worked examples on this page — Ulrich (1984) and Lee, Park & Schultz (2025) — are hand-built; the DOI field, the upload action, and all evaluation tables are static until the backend lands.

Open decisions

Whether the surface should accept a free-text title or only a DOI — CW recommends both, with DOI taking priority and free text becoming a search. What the surface does when a paper falls outside the Atlas corpus — CW recommends refusing the evaluation for Week 1 to avoid over-promising, while still suggesting related corpus papers. Whether the Path B VOI computation should report conditional VOI (what would this paper add given the existing corpus?) or topic-level VOI (how saturated is this topic?) — both are needed, and the VOI panel should clarify which it produces in each context.
